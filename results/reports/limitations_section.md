# Limitations and Future Work

While the proposed spatial-spectral hybrid pipeline demonstrates robust state-of-the-art denoising capabilities across complex Martian orbital datasets, several inherent limitations must be acknowledged to contextualize these findings. 

First, the comparative evaluation was constrained to a dataset of 11 physical CRISM scenes. Furthermore, because the chosen self-supervised baseline algorithm (N2N4M) inherently requires exactly 350 fixed spectral input features to execute its 1D U-Net architecture, paired statistical comparisons against N2N4M were structurally restricted to a subset of 5 L-sensor scenes. While the sample size is mathematically sufficient to extract highly significant non-parametric differences (e.g., Wilcoxon $p \approx 0.06$ for SAM, Paired $t$-test $p < 0.001$), future work should prioritize expanding the dataset pipeline to encompass hundreds of spectrally diverse physical scenes to stress-test generalization.

Second, the evaluation of any denoising algorithm on real orbital data is fundamentally limited by the absence of pristine ground truth. Unlike synthetic noise injections, it is physically impossible to obtain a "clean" counterpart of a real CRISM observation. Consequently, our evaluation rigorously relied on no-reference spatial and spectral metrics (such as spatial Signal-to-Noise Ratio and Spectral Angle Mapper derived from median scene profiles). However, no-reference metrics carry inherent vulnerabilities; as explicitly demonstrated during the N2N4M evaluation, an algorithm that aggressively smooths an image will artificially inflate spatial SNR to mathematically absurd levels (SNR $> 6000$) due to the spatial variance collapsing toward zero. To mitigate this limitation, our methodology necessitates a strict holistic evaluation where spatial SNR improvements are only considered scientifically valid if they are coupled with high spectral Pearson correlations and low SAM angular errors.

Future development of this architecture will address the integration of additional sensor modalities. While the Proposed Pipeline seamlessly adapts to both CRISM L-sensor and S-sensor configurations without requiring structural weight retraining—a distinct advantage over fixed-architecture deep learning baselines—its efficacy should be empirically validated on other hyperspectral planetary instruments, such as the Moon Mineralogy Mapper ($M^3$) and the EMIT spectrometer. Adapting the hybrid framework to dynamically handle varying spatial-spectral resolutions will further establish its utility as an instrument-agnostic tool for planetary spectroscopy.
